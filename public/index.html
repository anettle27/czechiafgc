The challenge

The Olympics of robotics.

Every year, each country sends a single national team of high school students to design, build and program a robot, then compete against the rest of the world. It's the largest youth robotics event on the planet, and it moves to a new host country every year.

The 2026 game

Igniting Innovation.

The 2026 game in Incheon was built around one of the planet's growing problems: wildfires.

Robots against wildfire

The 2026 theme, "Igniting Innovation", asks teams to build robots around wildfire prevention, mitigation and recovery. Every alliance plays the same game, so the difference comes down to design, strategy and teamwork.

Our ten-student team spent the season designing, building and programming the robot, then took it to Korea to play 11 ranking matches against the world. Read how it went on our past competitions page.

How the game works

Each match lasts 2 minutes 30 seconds on a 7 × 7 m field. When the match starts, 500 orange foam balls, the WILDFIRE, pour out across the floor. Six robots play at once: two red and blue regional alliances of three countries each, and all six together as one global alliance.

Official game trailer from FIRST Global

  1. 1

    Contain the wildfire

    Robots scoop up balls and drop them into their alliance's suppression unit, a tower about 2 m tall. Each ball is worth 1 point. Robots can also push balls into the fire shield in their corner, where the human player loads them into a chute and releases them back to the robot.

  2. 2

    Put it out together

    Human players take balls out of the fire shields and throw them into the shared extinguisher. Every ball there earns 1 point for all six teams. If the extinguisher ends up holding more balls than either suppression unit, everyone gets a +10 knockdown bonus.

  3. 3

    Climb the brace

    At the end, robots climb the brace, a 6.4 m steel pipe rising to almost 2 m. The higher they get, the bigger the multiplier on their suppression unit points: ×0.05 for touching it, then ×0.10, ×0.20 or ×0.30 per robot for Zones 1, 2 and 3. Lifting a partner robot off the floor earns 25 points per robot carried.

  4. 4

    Rise to Zone 3 together

    If four or more robots finish hanging in Zone 3, all six teams earn a Coopertition bonus: 10 points for four robots, 25 for five and 40 for all six.

Alliance score

(suppression unit balls × climb multiplier) + partner climbs + extinguisher balls + knockdown bonus + Coopertition bonus
